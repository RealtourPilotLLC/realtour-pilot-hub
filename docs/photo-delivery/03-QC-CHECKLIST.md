# REALTOUR PILOT: MASTER PHOTOGRAPHY QUALITY CONTROL CHECKLIST

> Source of truth for the QC engine. Encoded rule by rule in `02-QC-RULES-REGISTRY.md` / `qc-rules.seed.json`. If this file changes, regenerate the registry.

**Purpose:** Automated inspection of finished real estate photography before client delivery.

**Objective:** Identify property-preparation problems, technical photography defects, editing mistakes, AI-generated artifacts, missing deliverables, and inconsistencies before photographs are released to clients.

**Sources:** Realtour Pilot Creative Specialist In-field Project Checklist, photographer training materials, Slack conversations with Kyle Smith, historical client revision requests, and additional recommended photography standards.

## 1. GENERAL PROPERTY PREPARATION

Inspect every photograph for the following:

- Trash cans or recycling bins visible unnecessarily.
- Garbage bags or loose trash.
- Cardboard boxes, shipping boxes, and moving supplies.
- Cleaning supplies, including sprays, bottles, mops, brooms, and buckets.
- Vacuum cleaners or cleaning equipment.
- Loose extension cords, charging cables, and exposed wires.
- Personal photographs containing recognizable people.
- Visible personal documents, mail, addresses, or sensitive information.
- Shoes, slippers, and loose footwear.
- Coats, purses, backpacks, and personal accessories.
- Pet bowls, toys, leashes, crates, and litter boxes.
- Children's toys and play equipment creating clutter.
- Laundry baskets, dirty clothing, and loose clothing.
- Unnecessary countertop and furniture clutter.
- Open cabinets, drawers, or closets that should be closed.
- Awkwardly positioned or partially open doors.
- Crooked rugs, furniture, and decorative objects.
- Wrinkled or poorly arranged textiles.
- Crooked lampshades and light fixtures.
- Uneven or poorly positioned curtains.
- Inconsistent blind positions within a room.
- Unnecessary objects obstructing architectural features.
- Visible construction or painting materials.
- Temporary signage, stickers, or labels.
- Green, blue, or yellow painter's tape on windows, doors, frames, cabinets, or handles.
- Other distracting personal belongings.
- Visible people or pets unless intentionally included and approved.

**Standard:** Properties should appear clean, organized, intentionally presented, and ready for marketing. Flag distracting objects that could reasonably have been moved before photographing.

## 19. ISSUE SEVERITY CLASSIFICATION

### CRITICAL: Hold Delivery

Examples:

- Significant blur or unusable photographs.
- Repeated sensor dust throughout a gallery.
- Materially inaccurate property colors.
- Important architecture cropped off.
- AI edits materially misrepresenting property features.
- Missing essential photographs or purchased services.
- Missing requested features.
- Privacy-sensitive information visible.
- Wrong-property photographs.
- Corrupted or missing deliverables.
- Significant sky or architecture masking failures.
- Unresolved major client revision requests.

### MAJOR: Correction or Human Review Required

Examples:

- Visible trash cans.
- Open toilet seats.
- Prominent toiletries.
- Obvious clutter.
- Painter's tape.
- Garden hoses.
- Distracting cars.
- Noticeable glare.
- Crooked architectural lines.
- Excessive fisheye distortion.
- Poor HDR processing.
- Inconsistent white balance.
- Unrealistic skies.
- Obvious object-removal artifacts.
- Poor furniture presentation.
- Cobwebs or debris.
- Poorly chosen primary photo angles.

### MINOR: Nonblocking Flag

Examples:

- Slightly uneven pillows.
- Small decorative distractions.
- Minor rug misalignment.
- Slightly inconsistent curtain positions.
- Small, non-distracting clutter.
- Ordinary storage in a garage.
- Minor stylistic differences.
- Subtle composition improvements.

## 20. AUTOMATION WORKFLOW

### PASS 1: Individual Photo Inspection

Analyze each finished image for:

1. Property clutter and preparation issues.
2. Visible unwanted objects.
3. Dust spots.
4. Reflections and glare.
5. Technical focus and sharpness.
6. Exposure and lighting.
7. Perspective distortion.
8. Cropping and composition.
9. Color accuracy concerns.
10. HDR processing defects.
11. Sky replacement problems.
12. AI-generated editing artifacts.
13. Privacy-sensitive information.

### PASS 2: Full-Gallery Inspection

Analyze photographs together for:

1. Inconsistent wall colors.
2. Inconsistent cabinet colors.
3. Inconsistent flooring colors.
4. Inconsistent white balance.
5. Repeated dust spots.
6. Duplicate images.
7. Missing room angles where determinable.
8. Inconsistent visual quality.
9. Uncorrected versions mixed with revised photographs.
10. Overall presentation consistency.

### PASS 3: Original Versus Edited Inspection

When original images are available:

1. Compare colors.
2. Compare cropping.
3. Compare architectural features.
4. Compare room proportions.
5. Detect unintended alterations.
6. Detect inappropriate AI cleanup.
7. Verify object-removal integrity.
8. Flag unexplained changes to permanent features.

### PASS 4: Order and Delivery Validation

1. Retrieve property details.
2. Retrieve purchased services.
3. Retrieve photographer instructions.
4. Retrieve requested features.
5. Compare the gallery against required coverage.
6. Confirm ordered photo count or package requirements.
7. Verify all revisions.
8. Confirm finished files are uploaded.
9. Hold delivery if critical issues remain.

## 21. REQUIRED OUTPUT FOR EACH QC ISSUE

Each issue should include:

- Photograph filename.
- Property/project identifier.
- Issue category.
- Specific issue detected.
- Severity: Critical, Major, or Minor.
- Confidence score.
- Location of issue within the photograph.
- Bounding box or highlighted region when possible.
- Brief explanation.
- Recommended corrective action.
- Correction responsibility: Photographer, Editor, Admin, or Human Review.
- Whether the issue can reasonably be fixed in post-production.
- Whether reshooting may be necessary.
- Review status.
- Link to the affected photograph.
- Original and edited comparison when available.

Group repeated problems across photographs so staff do not receive dozens of duplicate tickets for the same underlying issue.

## 22. AUTOMATION BEHAVIOR AND GUARDRAILS

- Prioritize objective defects over subjective styling preferences.
- Use room-aware rules rather than applying every rule to every room.
- Do not automatically fail photographs simply because normal household objects exist.
- Distinguish permanent features from removable clutter.
- Never assume an actual defect may be digitally concealed without approval.
- Never assume an AI-generated replacement is accurate.
- Compare RAW files when material colors or architectural details are uncertain.
- Do not treat a confidence score as proof.
- Allow manual overrides with documented reasons.
- Use property-specific instructions to determine exceptions.
- Flag uncertain findings instead of presenting them as facts.
- Do not automatically order reshoots based solely on AI judgment.
- Do not automatically deliver galleries containing unresolved critical issues.
- Avoid requiring human approval for every minor preference.
- Track recurring defects by photographer, editor, property, and issue category.
- Preserve the original files for reference.
- Record which checks were performed and which were skipped because data was unavailable.

## FINAL QC STANDARD

A Realtour Pilot photo gallery is ready for delivery when:

- The property is presented professionally.
- There are no unresolved significant distractions.
- The photographs are sharp, balanced, correctly framed, and visually consistent.
- Architectural lines and proportions are natural.
- Actual property colors are represented accurately.
- Editing and AI enhancements do not materially misrepresent the home.
- All ordered photographs and requested features are accounted for.
- All necessary revisions have been completed.
- The final gallery has passed image-level, gallery-level, and order-level quality control.

**Ultimate goal:** Catch preventable photography and editing mistakes before the client sees them, reduce manual QC time, minimize revisions and reshoots, and maintain a consistent premium standard across every Realtour Pilot listing.
